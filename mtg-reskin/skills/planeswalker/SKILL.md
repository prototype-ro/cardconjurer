> Built on skills/art-template/SKILL.md.

This file adds planeswalker-specific art direction on top of the shared rules in `art-template/SKILL.md` (diegetic lighting, palette discipline, intricacy, framing, banned words, closing suffix, etc. — all apply unmodified). It does not repeat those rules.

**Planeswalker-specific override of the "Who" step:** art-template's Who/What/Why/Where brief (art-template/SKILL.md:11) treats "Who" as an open decision — many cards have no figure at all. Planeswalkers remove that decision entirely: the card *is* a specific, named, recurring character (Ajani, Jace, Chandra, Garruk, Ugin, and so on), never a generic figure or a stand-in. The figure is always present, always the dominant subject, and always identifiable as that specific character rather than "a mage" or "a warrior." There is exactly one compositional mode for this reason — planeswalkers don't have the mode-taxonomy branching the other type skills use, because the card type itself already answers the compositional question.

**Forward-reference — character-bible reuse:** planeswalkers are exactly the kind of card the naming/theme-bible skills (built in later tasks) will treat as a "named legendary character" needing a reusable description block, not a fresh physical description per prompt. `reference/user-prompts.md`'s five-prompt Ob Nixilis set (prompts #15, 16, 18, 19, with #17 as a deliberate mid-transformation exception) demonstrates the pattern: fix the character's physical description once — skin, wings, armor, helm, in that Ob Nixilis case — and repeat it near-verbatim across every prompt for that character, varying only the action clause and setting. A reskinned planeswalker will typically appear across multiple printings within a deck (an original card plus its walker-flip transformation, or simply multiple cards for the same named character across a multi-set cycle like the `fra`/`sos`/`tdm` Chandras and Garruks sampled below), and each printing should draw its physical description from that one fixed bible rather than re-inventing the character's appearance card to card. When a reskin deck's naming/theme-bible skill exists, route a planeswalker's physical description through it before writing the prompt; until then, write that bible paragraph once per character within the deck's own working notes and paste it into every prompt for that character.

## Mode 1 — Iconic Pose, Ability in Use

**When it applies:** every planeswalker, without exception.

**Decisive moment:** the character is always shown mid-cast — caught in the exact instant one of the card's own loyalty abilities is visibly firing, never at rest, never in a neutral standing portrait. Pick whichever printed ability (the +, 0, or − effect) gives the most visually concrete, nameable effect, and stage that specific effect as the diegetic light source per Rule 2 — the ability's glow, blast, or aura *is* the light in the scene, not a separate add-on. This satisfies art-template's Why test directly: the visual metaphor is not "a wizard casting a spell" in the abstract, it is *this* character using *this* printed ability.

Framing is always a static dominant-pose portrait — a single, centered, legible figure fully contained in frame per Rule 5, not an action shot of clashing forces or a wide establishing scene (that composition belongs to other card types, e.g. `battle`'s contested-territory framing). The character's face and the ability's effect are usually the two elements sharing the frame's center; per Rule 6, keep a visible margin of setting around the figure rather than an edge-to-edge close crop.

**Palette:** art-template Rule 4 caps every prompt at 2-3 named colors regardless of card type. For planeswalkers specifically, don't reach for one fixed universal palette — a card's own converted-mana colors (its color identity as printed) should drive the 2-3 named colors for that specific example, so a white planeswalker's prompt names warm gold/cream tones and a blue one names icy blue/slate tones, each within the same 2-3-color cap. Multicolor planeswalkers get one palette that blends both of their colors' associated tones rather than doubling up to 4+ colors — still pick only 2-3 total.

### Worked examples (pooled across colors — no color split needed at this volume)

1. *(White, based on Ajani Resolute.)* Ajani, a golden-maned leonin caught mid-roar, one taloned hand held low and cupped where a slowly turning ring of pure gold light hovers — the visible shape of his life-gain ability — its warm glow crisping the fur and claws of that hand while a trio of stray flame-red energy trails streak past in the hazy violet-dusk background behind him, clearly a rival effect and not his own. Palette: warm gold, cream-white fur, dusky violet haze.
2. *(Blue, based on Jace, Reality Sculptor.)* A hooded figure stands with one arm raised into the center of a rippling circular blue portal that hangs open behind him like a torn seam in the sky, a snow-capped mountain range visible through the portal's far side as if his ability is folding two locations into one view — the portal's own cold light is the source, crisping the white piping on his coat and fading to soft blue-grey haze at the frame's edges. Palette: icy blue, bone white, charcoal-black hood.
3. *(Black/green, based on Professor Dellian Fel.)* A sharp-dressed vampire-lich lecturer stands three-quarter to camera in a fog-choked greenhouse-turned-lab, one clawed hand raised with a curling tendril of sickly violet-green life-drain energy spooling off his fingertips toward an unseen target — the tendril's own bioluminescent glow is the light source, throwing green-black highlights across his high collar while the lab recedes into damp haze behind him. Palette: deep viridian green, near-black, violet-magenta accent.
4. *(Red, based on Chandra, Torch of Defiance.)* Chandra sprints forward through a narrow rock cleft, both arms trailing raw fire that wreathes her legs and one outstretched hand where a fresh gout of flame is just igniting — the fire is the sole light source, crisping her armor's edge nearest the flame while the cave walls behind her fall into cool blue-black shadow by contrast. Palette: molten orange-red, ember gold, cool slate-blue shadow.
5. *(Green, based on Garruk, Curse Breaker.)* A hulking, half-feral hunter raises a bone-and-antler haft overhead, its head wreathed in a crackling sickly-green rune-light that is clearly his own magic rather than a natural glow, the light throwing hard highlights across his fur mantle and tusked jaw while an icy grey wilderness recedes into flat haze behind him. Palette: bone white, moss-green glow, slate grey.

**Sampling note:** planeswalkers sampled at this depth came from Scryfall's `t:planeswalker year>=2010 -is:digital -is:promo` query, `unique=art`, sorted by release date descending — all five worked examples above are verified via the Scryfall set API to come from mainline expansion or commander sets (no Universes Beyond crossover). Planeswalkers are one of the card types with the heaviest Universes Beyond crossover presence on Scryfall (Marvel- and other IP-themed planeswalker-like cards turn up often in a naive search), so every candidate here was checked against its set code before inclusion; none needed to be excluded from this particular set of five, but that's a property of this sample, not a guarantee — recheck set codes on any future resample. A sixth colorless example (Ugin, Eye of the Storms) was also viewed and confirms the same mode with no new convention — a static portrait lit by his own colorless-mana ability — so it isn't written up as a separate worked example.
