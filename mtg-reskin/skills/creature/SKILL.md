> Built on skills/art-template/SKILL.md.

This file adds creature-specific art direction on top of the shared rules in `art-template/SKILL.md` (diegetic lighting, palette discipline, intricacy, framing, banned words, closing suffix, etc. — all apply unmodified). It does not repeat those rules; it adds what's specific to creatures: per-color visual registers, per-subtype anatomy and identity rules, a hybrid-subtype rule, and a compositional-mode taxonomy.

Everything below is distilled from a full Scryfall sampling pass (creature art printed 2010+) — see `reference/corpus-notes.md` for the exact queries, counts, and an honest accounting of what was actually viewed vs. counted, including several cells where Universes Beyond crossover cards (Marvel, Final Fantasy, Assassin's Creed, etc.) had to be caught and discounted mid-sampling. A few cells (flagged inline below) are thin and should be treated as a starting hypothesis rather than settled house style.

## Subtype fidelity is mandatory

A creature's subtype is not flavor text — it is the single most load-bearing visual instruction in the prompt. Per art-template Rule 3 (Intricacy), a generic "detailed" or "ornate" creature does not satisfy the corpus's own proven standard, and the same principle applies one level up: a generic "demon" or "angel" that could be swapped for any other demon or angel without anyone noticing has failed just as badly as a generic "ornate" object. Every creature prompt must include at least the signature features named in that subtype's section below — they are non-negotiable, not optional flourish. If a subtype's signature features are dropped in favor of generic fantasy-creature description, the prompt has not actually depicted that subtype.

# Part 1 — Color Conventions

## White

**Palette menu (pick 2-3 per art-template Rule 4, not all 4):** bone-white/pale ivory armor, dried-blood crimson, gunmetal silver, warm parchment-gold trim. A cool-white figure is typically set against a hot red or ochre ground, so pairing the white/ivory base with one of crimson or gold (not both) plus silver is the usual split.

**Materials/textures:** fluted bone-white plate with visible rivet seams, starched pleated linen collars, lacquered swirl-pattern cloth panels, fur-trimmed cloak collars — rigid geometric plate over soft draped cloth.

**Lighting:** a single hard rim-light from above/behind (halo-adjacent backlight), otherwise flat cold daylight and hard specular metal highlights rather than any glow.

**Emotional register:** rigid, unyielding, often masked — protective but cold and procedural, closer to an inquisitor than a healer.

**Worked examples:**
1. A bone-white sentinel kneels at a shattered gate, one fluted-plate arm raised to catch a collapsing archway, hairline cracks glowing faint gold under cold noon backlight.
2. A masked guardian's silver gauntlet snatches a falling relic mid-drop, the relic's glow throwing a narrow cone across the mask's blank eye-slits.
3. A knight plants a standard into cracked stone under flat grey daylight, pauldron rivets visibly mismatched from field repair.

## Blue

**Palette menu (pick 2-3 per art-template Rule 4, not all 4):** deep indigo/teal, cyan-white spell glow, violet-magenta swirl, gold trim as the one warm note. The indigo/teal base plus the cyan-white spell glow is the load-bearing pair; add either the violet-magenta swirl or the gold trim as the third, not both.

**Materials/textures:** wet-look serpentine coils wound in gold wire, etched brass alchemical apparatus with glass vials, rune-carved helm plating, a spiraling arcane portal with a ghostly architectural silhouette inside.

**Lighting:** light sourced from the spell effect itself — a portal or floating reagent-glass lights the face while everything outside recedes into blue-black haze.

**Emotional register:** composed and calculating, mid-manipulation — control over chaos, not serenity.

**Worked examples:**
1. A robed archivist reaches into a widening rift, fingers lit cyan by the portal's inner glow, the far edge dissolving into violet static.
2. A serpentine spellcaster's gold-wound tail catches light from a cracked flask floating at her palm, sickly blue-green light crisp on her collarbone and fading to indigo past her shoulders.
3. A mage closes her hand around a floating thought-shard, its light throwing sharp cool highlights on bent brass instruments strapped to her back.

## Black

**Palette menu (pick 2-3 per art-template Rule 4, not all 4):** oxidized rust-red (not fire-red), near-black shadow, sickly bone-pale skin, occasional acid-green (toxic demon-flesh highlight). Rust-red plus near-black shadow is the constant pair; add either the bone-pale skin tone or the acid-green accent as the third, not both.

**Materials/textures:** exposed ribcage-like armor fused to flesh, severed/held trophy heads, jagged bone spurs erupting through skin, chained pendant jewelry on rotted collars.

**Lighting:** low guttering light from below, carving harsh underlight onto jaws and brows, throwing everything above the chin into shadow.

**Emotional register:** predatory and triumphant over loss — trophies brandished, mid-ritual screaming, gloating dominance rather than abstract "evil."

**Worked examples:**
1. A ribbed-armor priestess holds a rival's severed head aloft, torchlight from below catching blood on her collarbone, the crowd lost in cave-black shadow.
2. A chained ritualist strains against an altar, ember-glow underlighting a jaw stretched wide, cracked bone spurs pushing through his own shoulder skin.
3. A demon lord grins with a torch in hand, acid-green glow reflecting wet off cracked obsidian ruins fading to pure black past the torch's reach.

## Red

*(Low-confidence: only 3 legitimate non-crossover images informed this section — Edgar Markov, Fury, Bello, Bard of the Brambles. See `reference/corpus-notes.md`. Treat as a starting hypothesis.)*

**Palette:** ember orange-red, near-black charcoal, one desaturated accent (pink/magenta sash, or acid-green magic) cutting across the warm base.

**Materials/textures:** spiked ram-horn helm, frayed beaded necklace and tattered fabric strips, a starched white collar against a black coat, a tattered orange cloak with strung berries.

**Lighting:** ember-glow from an unseen source at the figure's flank, hard orange rim-light against charcoal-black.

**Emotional register:** not uniformly berserk — coiled, barely-restrained aggression in one figure sits alongside colder, composed menace in another.

**Worked examples:**
1. A horned ember-elemental crouches, one claw raised, unseen fire below throwing a copper rim on horn and jaw, a frayed pink sash whipping at the waist, the far side already lost to shadow.
2. A red-eyed noble leans into candlelight at a black table, blood wet at his mouth, a starched white collar catching the only clean highlight.
3. A cloaked wanderer raises a berry-strung talisman as green spell-light crosses one hand and an unrelated ember-red glow lights the other side of his cloak — the two lighting qualities kept distinct.

## Green

**Palette:** moss-green and loamy brown, muted violet-grey shadow, warm honey-gold jewelry accents.

**Materials/textures:** overlapping fur pelts on humanoid-animal figures, bark-ridged horn/spine growths, hammered-gold circlets and leaf-shaped pauldrons, glowing sap/amber orbs held in claws.

**Lighting:** filtered canopy light in soft green-gold shafts, or a held bioluminescent/sap-glow object as the only warm accent in cool grove shadow.

**Emotional register:** communal and mobilizing rather than solitary — leader figures flanked by crowds of smaller kin.

**Worked examples:**
1. A squirrel chieftain vaults forward with violet spell-glow trailing off one claw, kin surging behind through dappled canopy light.
2. A fox-eared shaman cradles a molten sap-amber orb lighting her gold diadem from below, moss-slick bark receding into cool shadow past her shoulder.
3. A bark-skinned colossus pushes through undergrowth, canopy light breaking in gold shafts across ridged spine-growths, lower limbs lost to loam-dark shadow.

## Multicolor

*(Rebuilt without excluded Sauron/Mary Read and Anne Bonny — based on Edgar Markov, Ms. Bumbleflower, Bello, Bard of the Brambles, Hazel of the Rootbloom.)*

**Palette:** paired, deliberately distinct registers rather than a blend — gothic red-black-white contrast, or acid-green magic against warm amber fur and orange cloth.

**Materials/textures:** a starched pleated white collar against a black vampiric coat, fur-trimmed pastel robes with gold buttons, a tattered orange cloak strung with berries, a gold filigree diadem worn over fox fur.

**Lighting:** two physically distinct sources kept visually separate — per art-template Rule 2's two-effects-distinct clause, this is the color where that clause matters most.

**Emotional register:** a composed/regal exterior sitting over a feral undertone, or a small creature elevated to heroic status through ornament.

**Worked examples:**
1. A vampiric noble leans into candlelight, the warm flame catching his composed jaw while his eyes carry a separate, colder red glow the candle doesn't explain.
2. A raccoon druid channels acid-green spell light from one claw while a warm ember-lit talisman glows independently at his chest, the two colors never blending.
3. A woodland herald in fur-trimmed pastel robes raises a paw as her gold flower-crown catches warm daylight, while a floating amber spell-orb throws its own separate ember glow across her other hand.

## Colorless

**Palette menu (pick 2-3 per art-template Rule 4, not all 4):** brushed steel/pale platinum, aged verdigris bronze, glacier-blue ice, warm gold filigree in temple settings. These sort into two registers — cold-mechanical (steel/platinum + glacier-blue) or warm-relic (verdigris bronze + gold filigree) — pick 2-3 from within one register rather than mixing both wholesale.

**Materials/textures:** articulated ball-jointed chrome limbs, engraved swirling bronze/gold filigree over a construct's hull, branching crystalline ice-spikes, riveted industrial plating streaked with corrosion.

**Lighting:** a self-lit internal core or eye as the only saturated color against neutral overcast sky or plain stone architecture.

**Emotional register:** inert-until-triggered menace — dormant machinery or geological stillness with one glowing "tell."

**Worked examples:**
1. A dormant bronze colossus half-buried in temple rubble has a single veined-blue socket flickering awake, stone receding into flat overcast haze.
2. An articulated chrome seraph unfolds jointed wing-struts in a bare courtyard, brushed steel catching only flat daylight, no other light source present.
3. A branching crystalline ice-growth erupts from a glacier field, its own inner light the sole color against a bleached white-grey mountain backdrop.

# Part 2 — Subtype Conventions

## Angel

**Anatomy/silhouette:** bipedal humanoid, adult proportions, large paired wings at the shoulder blades spread wide behind the figure. Frontal/three-quarter standing or hovering pose, arms often extended outward. Armor worn over the body, not replacing it.

**Signature features:** (1) individually delineated flight feathers, not a membrane; (2) a raised weapon-tipped staff/polearm functioning as a second "wing" line; (3) a halo-adjacent light source at or behind the head.

**Vs. Horror (nearest neighbor):** Angel keeps bilateral symmetry, a single readable head, anatomically correct wings. The moment wing structure or facial symmetry breaks (asymmetric wing damage, a second mouth/eye off-axis, a jagged/inverted halo), it has crossed into Horror. Symmetry-intact is the litmus test.

**Worked examples:**
1. An armored figure hovers above cracked marble, both wings mid-downstroke, feather tips catching light from a rose window, polearm held level as if just driven into the ground.
2. Close on a raised gauntlet gripping a sunburst-headed staff, the near wing draping into the lower frame, the far wing lost in backlight haze.
3. Three-quarter shot from below the sternum, breastplate catching hard light, both wings spread flat against smoke-filled sky, feather edges silhouette black against orange glow.

## Demon

**Anatomy/silhouette:** heavyset humanoid/ogre-scaled, crouched/coiled/leaning forward, weight on bent legs. Horns as skull-growths. Wings when present are membrane/bat-type, folded or half-furled.

**Signature features:** (1) an oversized gaping mouth with disproportionate teeth; (2) asymmetric/branching horns as skull-growths; (3) visible internal glow or veining in the skin (lava-crack, glowing seams).

**Vs. Devil:** Demons are large and physically overwhelming with a mute/bestial expression. Devils are small-to-human-scaled, wiry, sly/mischievous. Scale and expression, not horn count, is the separator.

**Worked examples:**
1. A crouched red-skinned demon plants fists into scorched stone, jaw unhinged wider than its skull, cracked-lava seams brightening where muscle flexes.
2. A hooded demon reaches toward a red archway, engraved bone-white antlers sweeping back, only its clawed hand and head-top lit by the arch's glow.
3. A vampiric demon-woman erupts from torn white cloth-like tendrils, jaw stretched into a scream, red eyes the only warm color against cold blue-white.

## Devil

**Anatomy/silhouette:** small-to-human scale, slender/wiry, shown mid-gesture rather than mid-combat. Small bat-membrane wings when present. A thin prehensile tail is a recurring element.

**Signature features:** (1) a close-lipped/smirking expression with arched eyebrows — clever, not feral; (2) small backswept horns high on the forehead; (3) a social/character prop (glasses, a teacup, a vest) — dressed like a character, not just a monster.

**Vs. Demon: see above** — Demon's Vs. line is the reciprocal statement of this same boundary (scale and expression, not horn count).

**Worked examples:**
1. A red bespectacled devil perches on a shoulder, clawed hand cupped near an ear, tail curled around a teacup handle.
2. A horned vest-and-shirtsleeves devil swings a barbed weapon overhead in a tavern brawl, tail whipping for balance, grin as prominent as the weapon.
3. A crouched toad-mouthed devil in a wide-brimmed hat picks at its teeth with a claw in a bloodstained stone cell.

## Vampire

*(Astarion, the Decadent excluded as Baldur's Gate 3/D&D Universes Beyond content; based on Edgar Markov, Bloodthirsty Conqueror, Bloodletter of Aclazotz.)*

**Anatomy/silhouette:** human proportions, formal/composed presentation — portrait-bust framing rather than dynamic action; when full figures appear, torsos emerge from a swirling mass of subordinate winged faces and armor pieces. Aristocratic dress even in a plain portrait (ruffled high collar, dark formal coat, a circlet or pendant).

**Signature features:** (1) a single thin trickle of blood at the mouth/chin paired with visible fangs — the wound detail sells "vampire," not fangs alone; (2) a widow's-peak hairline or centrally-parted long hair framing a pale, sharp-cheekboned face; (3) a crescent moon or night-sky backdrop.

**Vs. corrupted/horror register:** a vampire keeps full human facial proportions and an aristocratic frame; once the face stretches into a screaming rictus with solid red/white eyes and a torn tendril-material body, it has crossed into a vampire-demon/horror hybrid — reserve that for boss-tier or corrupted-bloodline cards, not the baseline.

**Worked examples:**
1. A silver-haired vampire lord's portrait is lit only by an unseen candle below frame, one bead of blood sliding from the corner of his mouth, a pale crescent moon fixed just over his shoulder.
2. A white-haired figure rises at the center of a mass of smaller bat-winged faces and golden ornamental armor pieces, red drapery the only warm accent against the cold pink-silver swarm.
3. A screaming, corrupted vampire-thing claws forward out of shredded pale cloth, fangs bared, red eyes the only saturated color in an otherwise cold blue-white palette.

## Zombie

**Anatomy/silhouette:** human proportions with compromised posture (hunched, lolling head, dragging/clambering gait). Clothing intact but soiled/period-specific — the clothing tells you who the person was before death.

**Signature features:** (1) localized rot as patchy discoloration or an exposed cheekbone, not full skeletal exposure; (2) cloudy/off-center/crossed eyes; (3) a graveyard prop cluster grounding origin.

**Vs. Skeleton:** the dividing line is flesh-coverage. If skin/muscle covers the dominant mass, it's Zombie; if bone is the dominant visible material, it's Skeleton, regardless of pose. A bare skull automatically reads Skeleton, not Zombie.

**Worked examples:**
1. A gray-green figure in a rotted funeral suit claws from a broken coffin lid, one crossed cataract-white eye catching moonlight, fresh soil sliding off shoulders.
2. A zombie in a stained ice-cream-vendor uniform pushes a cart past a gate, exposed green-gray cheekbone visible near the jaw.
3. A moon-lit zombie woman drags a figure by the collar between headstones, matted colorless hair, torn pink funeral fabric.

## Skeleton

**Anatomy/silhouette:** human skeletal proportions, bare bone throughout, full ribcage, visible joint sockets, no flesh. Posture ranges from regal/upright to loose-limbed.

**Signature features:** (1) a fully exposed skull with visible tooth row and empty/glowing eye sockets; (2) bone-colored non-muscled limbs with visible joint articulation; (3) regalia draped over bare bone (a crown, a robe) producing a "royalty of the dead" silhouette.

**Vs. Zombie:** see above — bone dominant, not flesh.

**Worked examples:**
1. A crowned skeletal king raises both arms, pale energy whipping off fingertips, ribcage visible beneath a half-buttoned scale-mail robe.
2. A gaunt hooded skeleton hunches forward, one hand extended palm-up, jaw slightly parted.
3. A red-eyed hooded skeletal thief bursts from a sack of coins, bony fingers splayed, caught mid-leap.

## Spirit

*(Wan Shi Tong, Librarian excluded as Avatar: The Last Airbender content; based on Simian Spirit Guide, Elvish Spirit Guide, Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm.)*

**Anatomy/silhouette:** variable base form (primate, dryad, serpentine) with a soft, dissolving edge — the outline breaks into vapor-strokes or ink-brush cloud shapes rather than a hard contour. Calm or mid-drift poses.

**Signature features:** (1) thin trailing wisps of colored vapor peeling directly off the extremities; (2) a palette that partially blends into the background so the figure reads as an emanation of its setting; (3) faint echo/afterimage marks suggesting slight out-of-phase existence.

**Vs. Horror:** Spirit stays gentle and watchful, its "wrongness" reading as ethereal dissolution — never violent bodily distortion. A second mouth, asymmetric tearing, or aggressive fangs/claws crosses into Horror.

**Worked examples:**
1. A gray-furred spirit-monkey sits cross-legged atop a mossy stone, twin trails of red-gold vapor pouring from its dangling hand into a striped sky.
2. A green dryad-like spirit drifts past a cluster of white doves, her hair rendered as looping ink-lines blending directly into the leaf pattern behind her.
3. A jade-scaled serpentine dragon-spirit coils in on itself, mane and tail dissolving into ink-brush cloud shapes rather than closing into a hard outline.

## Horror

*(Sauron, the Dark Lord excluded as a conservative Lord of the Rings judgment call; based on Mondrak, Glory Dominus; Hexhaven Invigorator; Solphim, Mayhem Dominus.)*

**Anatomy/silhouette:** no fixed body plan — a symmetric mass built from asymmetric/excessive parts: a single central maw or eye ringed by matching secondary shapes in radial or bilateral repetition, like a heraldic crest gone wrong. Fused/compound bodies.

**Signature features:** (1) a single dominant orifice dead-center as the visual anchor; (2) matched limb/wing pairs arranged radially that don't belong to any real animal; (3) fused, mismatched creature parts sharing one body.

**Vs. Spirit/Angel:** Horror is radial excess and violent asymmetry breaking a "should-be-normal" template — an Angel's wing doubling unnaturally, a face growing a second mouth. Keep true Spirits soft-edged and non-threatening; keep true Angels bilaterally correct.

**Worked examples:**
1. A bat-winged mass with a single fanged maw ringed by four smaller horned faces, wings and horn-pairs mirrored perfectly.
2. A radiant insectoid horror raises multiple thin limbs in a full circle around its crowned head, its face a smooth pale mask notched into a heraldic starburst.
3. A snake-headed plant-thing bursts through broken glass shards, twin serpentine heads snapping from a single stalk-neck, dripping venom from both jaws.

## Eldrazi

**Anatomy/silhouette:** titanic scale, radially symmetric/tentacle-radiating builds — a central spined/crested "head" mass with tentacles/root-appendages spreading outward, rooted into cracked ground/void.

**Signature features:** (1) bone-white/pale chitin plating in jagged crown formations; (2) void/lightning-torn backgrounds; (3) a complete absence of a recognizable face — no eyes or mouth positioned conventionally.

**Vs. Phyrexian (nearest neighbor — both "cosmic-horror biomechanical"):** Eldrazi are organic-void: boneless, tentacular, colorless-to-purple/gray, radiating from a formless center, no mechanical parts, no bilateral human-derived symmetry. Phyrexian retains a legible bilateral chassis with visible seams/blades/metal fused into flesh — Phyrexian always looks like something was once whole and has been augmented; Eldrazi looks like it was never anything but itself.

**Worked examples:**
1. A colossal crested white-boned mass hovers rooted to nothing, thin tentacles whipping below, blue lightning tearing the sky.
2. A skeletal-winged void creature crouches over broken red root-tendrils, a single central eye a smooth black orb, tentacle-fingers longer than its torso.
3. A tri-limbed spined horror straddles a cracked dais, red serpentine coils spilling from beneath a ribbed bone carapace.

## Phyrexian

**Anatomy/silhouette:** bilateral human/animal-derived chassis retained but visibly compleated — recognizable skeleton/posture augmented with metal plating, blades from limb-ends, asymmetric mechanical attachments.

**Signature features:** (1) a smooth featureless bone-white/chrome skull-mask, no visible eyes/mouth; (2) blades/hooks grown directly from forearms as continuous limb-extension, not held weapons (see art-template Rule 7); (3) visible pink/red muscle exposed between metal plates.

**Vs. Eldrazi:** see above.

**Worked examples:**
1. A bone-white praetor stands with both blade-tipped arms raised, a smooth beaked mask, pink sinew visible in chest-plating gaps.
2. A four-armed compleated horror crouches mid-stride, twin curved horn-blades, exposed red muscle striping torso between armor segments.
3. A regal sickle-armed figure haloed by a crescent blade-shape, gold trim on white bone plate, one hand ending in articulated claw-blades.

## Dragon

*(Smaug the Magnificent excluded as The Hobbit/Tolkien content; based on Ancient Copper Dragon, Twinflame Tyrant, Old Gnawbone.)*

**Anatomy/silhouette:** quadruped-with-wings — four clawed legs, a long sinuous neck, a long tail, bat-membrane wings at the shoulder. Elongated head with a distinct brow ridge/horn crown.

**Signature features:** (1) a visible horn/frill crown on the head, always present; (2) overlapping individual scales as a repeating texture; (3) an open-mouthed roar/breath-weapon moment as the default pose.

**Vs. Hydra:** Dragon = one head, one neck, four legs, wings, full body visible. Hydra = multiple heads/necks from a shared body mass, usually no wings, body often minimized/absent.

**Worked examples:**
1. A long-necked dragon lunges down out of a fire-lit sky toward a lone armored figure, spiked frills sweeping back, a ball of raw flame just leaving its jaws.
2. A steel-gray dragon dives from cloud cover, wings half-folded, red wing-membrane the only warm color against cold sky.
3. A weathered brown dragon perched on a rocky outcrop lets loose a curling plume of green-tinted fire, wing membrane rendered in loose ink-brush hatching.

## Sphinx

**Anatomy/silhouette:** winged humanoid torso fused to beast hindquarters/tail, large symmetric wings spread flat behind the head like an emblem. Frontal/static/ceremonial poses.

**Signature features:** (1) a radially symmetric feathered/crested headdress; (2) perfectly mirrored wing halves as compositional frame; (3) a serpentine/lion-derived tail visible below the hybrid torso.

**Vs. Angel:** an Angel's wings support an active flying pose on a fully human body; a Sphinx's wings are a static symmetrical frame behind a fused human-beast chassis, and the body below the waist is explicitly non-human.

**Worked examples:**
1. A gold-feathered sphinx sits motionless between carved pillars, wings spread flat symmetrically, taloned feet gripping stone.
2. A stylized sphinx's serpentine coils fill the lower frame, crested head tilted downward as if posing a riddle.
3. A weathered stone-colored sphinx crouches atop a dais, mirrored wings catching torchlight from symmetric braziers.

## Hydra

**Anatomy/silhouette:** a single shared body mass (often barely visible/cropped out) from which multiple long necks radiate outward, heads clustered in a loose ring/fan. No wings.

**Signature features:** (1) multiple independently-posed heads sharing one body; (2) dripping venom/ichor/drool from open jaws; (3) a faint color-coded glow per head in some designs.

**Vs. Dragon:** head count and body visibility — one head atop a fully visible winged body vs. multiple heads with the body minimized or absent.

**Worked examples:**
1. Five scaled heads converge nose-to-nose, jaws parted, each eye a different ember color, shared body lost below frame.
2. A jade-green hydra's heads recede in a row, venom stringing from the nearest jaw, flat ink-line scale texture.
3. A thorned plant-hydra's necks branch upward like a firework, one head mid-snap at a floating spark.

## Elemental

**Anatomy/silhouette:** a humanoid-adjacent armored figure where the "armor" is inseparable from elemental substance — a mask-like head that is the actual head, a body built from swirling material. Static, frontal, portrait-like poses.

**Signature features:** (1) a mask-like "face" that is the whole head, no skin/neck seam; (2) the home substance rendered as body material itself (literal water/flame/smoke, not a surrounding effect); (3) ornate symmetrical regalia detailing.

**Vs. Beast/Dinosaur:** Elemental's face/body is made of a single named substance with no organic skin anywhere; if substance stops and skin begins, it isn't an Elemental.

**Worked examples:**
1. A blue-skinned water elemental's braided hair is literal coiling water, gold bands wrapping the coils, a smooth expressionless mask face.
2. A red-plumed fire elemental's mane is actual flame around a segmented dark helm-face.
3. A masked shadow elemental raises both arms, its crest a spread of jagged blue-black feather-like smoke.

## Beast

**Anatomy/silhouette:** a quadruped/multi-limbed body of mismatched animal/mechanical parts, the generic-monster catch-all — physically overwhelming (thick limbs, low wide stance, disproportionate head/maw).

**Signature features:** (1) composite anatomy reading as assembled, not evolved; (2) an oversized maw/jaw disproportionate to the head; (3) visible mechanical/crystalline growths integrated into an organic frame.

**Vs. Elemental/Dinosaur:** Beast keeps organic flesh/scale/fur (unlike Elemental) but doesn't match a real-world animal lineage (unlike Dinosaur) — it's the "doesn't fit either bucket" residual.

**Worked examples:**
1. A spike-crowned red-eyed beast with a saurian head and insect-jointed limbs drags itself over ruins, a hooked mechanical claw catching torchlight.
2. A crouched chrome-limbed predator bristles with blue-glowing conduits, bat-like ears swept back.
3. A shaggy three-eyed quadruped with mismatched fur patches and an oversized underslung jaw stalks cracked earth, steam rising from spine vents.

## Dinosaur

**Anatomy/silhouette:** a recognizable theropod/raptor body plan — bipedal, long balancing tail, forward-leaning horizontal spine, small forelimbs, elongated jaw. Pack/paired poses recur.

**Signature features:** (1) a long horizontal tail held stiffly for counterbalance, always visible; (2) overlapping small scales/feather-quill texture, not flat plate-scales; (3) a jungle/prehistoric-terrain setting cue.

**Vs. Dragon/Beast:** Dinosaur keeps an anatomically real theropod silhouette (no wings, small forelimbs, horizontal spine/tail) and earthbound pack behavior. Dragon adds wings and a serpentine neck; Beast abandons real-animal anatomy for invented forms.

**Worked examples:**
1. Two raptors circle a fallen explorer, jaws slightly open, tails rigid, dense fern cover framing them.
2. A trio of crested tyrannosaurs roar in unison atop a stone dais, small forelimbs tucked tight, volcanic fire lighting the horizon.
3. A ghostly translucent theropod strides ahead of its own solid body, overlapping claw prints in cracked mud.

## Elf

**Anatomy/silhouette:** slender willowy human-scale build, long pointed ears sweeping back, smooth skin, long flowing hair. Lean torsos, composed/vertical poses even when militant.

**Signature features:** (1) long tapering ear points clear of the hairline; (2) living plant material worked into garments (bark vambraces, leaf-shoulder pieces, grown vine cording); (3) a woodland-crown accessory nested in the hair.

# Part 3 — Hybrid-Subtype Rule

When a creature has multiple creature subtypes (Angel Horror, Vampire Knight, Zombie Dragon, Human Cleric Wizard), the art must visibly combine the signature features of both — never let one subtype's identity erase the other's. A hybrid card's whole point is that it is legitimately both things at once; if the art reads as cleanly one subtype with no trace of the other, the hybrid has failed.

Use the boundary tests already established above as the practical check: each pair of "nearest-neighbor" subtypes in Part 2 comes with an explicit litmus test for where one crosses into the other. A hybrid card is exactly the case where you want the art to sit deliberately on both sides of that line at once, rather than resolving cleanly to one side:

- **Angel Horror:** per the Angel/Horror litmus test above (bilateral symmetry and a single readable head vs. radial excess and broken symmetry), an Angel Horror must keep enough angelic structure to read as an angel at a glance — individually delineated flight feathers, a halo-adjacent light, a legible head — while breaking symmetry in exactly the way Horror requires: one wing damaged asymmetrically where the other stays pristine, a second mouth appearing off-axis on an otherwise angelic face. Neither half should be diluted to make room for the other; both must be fully present and legible in the same frame.
- **Zombie Dragon:** per the Zombie/Skeleton flesh-coverage test above (applied by analogy — the same logic governs "how much of the base creature's material is replaced"), a Zombie Dragon keeps the Dragon's signature horn/frill crown, overlapping scale texture, and quadruped-with-wings silhouette intact, while applying Zombie's localized-rot-not-full-skeletal-exposure rule to the scaled hide itself: patchy discoloration and torn wing membrane, not a fully skeletal dragon (that would be a Skeleton Dragon, a different hybrid with different rules) and not an undamaged dragon with a "zombie" label doing no visual work.
- **Vampire Knight:** combine Vampire's aristocratic portrait cues (a widow's-peak hairline, a single blood trickle, a crescent-moon backdrop) with Knight's full plate and heraldic surcoat — the fangs and blood detail should sit on a face that is otherwise fully armored and combat-posed, not softened into an unarmored portrait just because Vampire's baseline convention favors portraiture.

The general rule this generalizes to: identify each component subtype's 1–3 signature features from Part 2, and require the prompt to explicitly name at least one signature feature from each subtype, staged so both are simultaneously visible rather than one crowding the other out of frame or out of the description.

# Part 4 — Compositional Modes

Every creature prompt should be built around one of these four modes. Pick the mode based on what the card's mechanic and rarity/role actually call for — don't default to the same mode for every card in a deck.

1. **Mid-combat action.** The creature is caught mid-strike, mid-cast, or mid-defense — a frozen instant of consequence, per art-template Rule 1's "What" clause (never a neutral idle pose). Use this for the majority of ordinary creatures, especially ones with combat-relevant abilities (first strike, deathtouch, combat tricks) where the mechanic itself implies an active moment.

2. **Static dominant pose.** A still, often frontal or symmetrical pose that reads as commanding rather than active — arms raised in address, a seated/enthroned stance, wings spread flat as a frame rather than mid-flap. Reserve this for legendary or singular creatures (a named planeswalker-adjacent legend, a boss-tier demon, a court noble) where stillness communicates authority and established power rather than a lack of danger.

3. **Transformation/corruption-in-progress.** The creature is caught mid-change — compleation, undeath, elemental fusion, a hybrid subtype's boundary made visible as an active process rather than a settled state. Use this for cards whose mechanic or flavor is explicitly about becoming something (a Phyrexian compleation card, a zombie mid-reanimation, an Eldrazi-corruption card) — the "why" per art-template Rule 1 should point directly at the transformation mechanic.

4. **Group/swarm composition.** Multiple instances of the same creature type staged together — a mob of goblins around a boss, a hydra's multiple heads, an insect swarm, a squad of soldiers in formation. Use this for tribal/token-generation mechanics, or any subtype (Goblin, Insect, Zombie, Elemental "cascade" effects) whose corpus convention already leans toward pack/mob staging per Part 2 above — don't isolate a single figure when the subtype's own visual identity is inherently collective.
