> Built on skills/art-template/SKILL.md.

This file adds sorcery-specific art direction on top of the shared rules in `art-template/SKILL.md` (diegetic lighting, palette discipline, intricacy, framing, banned words, closing suffix, etc. — all apply unmodified). It does not repeat those rules.

Like instants, sorceries have no meaningful subtypes to organize by, so this file organizes by **role** — mirroring the instant skill's structure, since the two types share most of the same roles at different speeds. Each role gets: the decisive-moment convention specific to that role, a per-color note on how the role's staging shifts with color identity, and 2-3 worked examples.

This is a **reduced-depth sample**, per the design spec's cost trade-off for non-creature types: one Scryfall pass per color (`t:sorcery c:<color> year>=2010 -is:digital -is:promo -st:funny`, `order=usd&dir=desc`), roughly 15-20 unique cards read per color, plus targeted oracle-text queries to fill gaps (e.g. `o:"destroy all creatures"` for board wipe, `o:"search your library for a land card"` for ramp, and direct name lookups for a few canonical staples — Cultivate, Rampant Growth, Reanimate, Lightning Bolt — whose printed art the price-sorted pass didn't surface first). A representative subset of resulting art was actually viewed (roughly 14 images across both instant and sorcery combined), not an exhaustive per-role-per-color grid. No separate corpus-notes.md file was produced at this depth, per the plan.

**Universes Beyond exclusions caught mid-sampling:** Their Name Is Death, Blight Grenade (Warhammer 40K, set `40k`); Everything Comes to Dust, Delete, Exterminate!, Nanogene Conversion (Doctor Who, set `who`); Avatar's Wrath, Spirit Water Revival, Redirect Lightning (Avatar: The Last Airbender, sets `tla`/`tle`); Flash Photography (Final Fantasy, set `fic`). As with the instant sample, set code alone wasn't a reliable signal — cards from the upcoming original set "Reality Fracture" (`fra`) looked crossover-adjacent by their evocative names (Vraska's Final Mercy, Kindred Judgment, Overwrite the Multiverse) but checked out as legitimate original Magic content once verified against the set itself, not licensed IP.

**Not every role spans every color evenly.** Counterspell is essentially absent at sorcery speed entirely (countering only works while a spell is on the stack, and virtually every counter effect is printed as an instant) — it's covered here only to say so, with no worked examples. Pump/protection is likewise rare as a sorcery — most combat tricks need instant speed to matter — so its section here is thin by design, not by oversight.

## Removal

**Decisive moment:** the instant of destruction itself, staged as visceral and internal rather than a clean external strike — since sorcery-speed removal can't ambush anything, its visual hook is usually that the destruction reads as more total or more final than an instant's version, not that it's sudden.

**Per-color notes:**
- **Black** (destroy target creature/planeswalker as internal combustion): the destruction erupts from *inside* the target — a chest cavity bursting open with fire or light from within — rather than an external blow landing. This reads as blacker and more total than a simple sword-strike.
- **White** (exile/destroy as slower, ceremonial judgment, when not scaled to a full board wipe): a beam or verdict-light descending onto a single target with the same "sanctioned judgment" register as white's instant-speed removal, but staged with more ritual weight — an audience, a dais, a procession — since sorcery speed allows the moment to be witnessed rather than snapped off in response to something.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Black, based on Dreadbore.)* A man's own chest has burned open along a jagged seam, molten orange-white light and fire pouring outward from within as he throws his head back mid-scream, stone corridor walls receding into cold blue shadow around the blast.
2. *(White, based on Damn.)* A single armored figure stands frozen at the center of a descending column of white light, the light's edge visibly dissolving the figure's silhouette from the head downward, onlookers at the frame's edges caught mid-flinch rather than mid-combat.

## Counterspell

Essentially absent at sorcery speed — counterspells target spells on the stack, which by definition requires instant speed to matter. The sampling pass found no legitimate sorcery-speed counter effects worth building a convention around. **See the Instant skill's Counterspell section** for the primary treatment; if a deck genuinely needs a sorcery-type "counter-adjacent" effect (a rare tax/denial effect), treat it as a variant of Removal or Board Wipe instead, staged as a static, already-resolved denial rather than a mid-flight interception.

## Board Wipe

**Decisive moment:** the wave of the effect crossing the *entire visible battlefield* in one continuous motion — every combatant caught in the same instant, not picked off one at a time. This is sorcery's signature role: unlike instant-speed removal, a board wipe telegraphs itself and lets the whole field react in the same frame.

**Per-color notes:**
- **White** (destroy all as purifying light): a single overwhelming light source — often a sun or sun-like glow — washes over the entire scene from one direction, with combatants reduced to dissolving silhouette/ash rather than gore. The verdict register from Removal above, scaled to the whole board.
- **Black** (destroy all as consuming dark/rot): the opposite direction of white's wipe — darkness or decay spreading outward from a central point rather than light washing in, consuming rather than purifying.
- **Red** (damage-based board wipe, often overlapping with Burn below): a single massive fire/energy column or blast crossing an entire skyline or village at once, distinguished from Removal-scale burn by literally engulfing multiple structures/figures in the same continuous effect rather than one target.
- **White (alternate register — land destruction):** a small number of white board wipes (Armageddon, Ravages of War) destroy all lands rather than all creatures — stage this as the ground itself failing (cracking, sinking, going barren) rather than a light-wash, since the target class is different.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(White, based on Wrath of God.)* A blinding white sun fills most of the upper frame, its light already washing across a battlefield where several armored figures are mid-collapse into loose ash and dust, only their weapons still holding a hard silhouette against the glare.
2. *(Black, based on Damnation.)* A creeping black-purple rot spreads outward from a central fissure in the ground, every visible creature in the frame caught at a different stage of dissolving into the same advancing dark, none of them granted a clean death.
3. *(Red, based on Bonfire of the Damned.)* A colossal fire-elemental figure looms over a burning village, a single vertical column of white-orange flame connecting its raised fist to the ground below and visibly engulfing multiple rooftops and fleeing figures at once, smoke obscuring everything past the column's light.

## Card Draw

**Decisive moment:** the moment of selection or acquisition made physical — a hand paging through, reaching for, or catching something specific, not a passive "person sitting with a full hand of cards."

**Per-color notes:**
- **Blue** (methodical, scholarly draw): a caster examining/selecting with visible tools (a lens, a candle, a ledger) — draw framed as diligence and technique rather than a burst of magic.
- **Black** (draw at a cost, mirroring the instant-speed convention): pair the card-gain with a visible cost in the same frame — a sacrificed creature's remains, a draining life-glow.

White, red, and green card draw were too thin in this sample to support a per-color convention — fall back on the shared decisive-moment description above for those colors rather than forcing a color-specific register.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Blue, based on Careful Study.)* A horned, robed scholar leans in close over an open book by candlelight, one hand holding the candle aloft and the other steadying a small lens against a glinting object on the desk, stacks of books receding into warm dark at the frame's edges.
2. *(Black, based on Stinging Study.)* A caster's outstretched hand siphons a thin stream of pale light directly from a commander-sized figure standing at their side, cards visibly coalescing out of the siphoned light before settling into the caster's other hand.

## Ramp

**Decisive moment:** land or mana bursting up *out of the ground itself* at the exact point of contact — sorcery-speed ramp is where this role's core visual identity lives (unlike the instant-speed version, which is mostly emergency mana rituals). The moment should be a hand or staff touching earth and the earth visibly answering, not a land card just appearing.

**Per-color note:** overwhelmingly green — ramp as a role belongs to green more than any other color in the sample. Palette: warm loam-brown earth meeting a cooler green-gold magical light at the point of contact, the light's glow the only saturated color in an otherwise earthy frame.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Based on Cultivate.)* A close hand, rings catching warm light, touches cracked dry earth at exactly the moment pale green light and new sprouting vines erupt from beneath the fingertips, the surrounding leaves and soil sharp near the hand and softening into loose green haze at the frame's edges.
2. *(Based on Rampant Growth/Sylvan Scrying, land-fetch register.)* A druid kneels at a forest's edge with a palm flat against root-tangled ground, a faint outline of an unseen distant mountain or plain briefly visible as a translucent overlay in the dirt before it — the land being called forth rather than already present.

## Tutor

**Decisive moment:** as with the instant version, the search rendered as physically *reaching into* something — a crowd, a void, an infernal presence — rather than a card materializing cleanly. Sorcery-speed tutors in the sample skewed toward larger, more ceremonial searches (fetching a creature onto the battlefield directly, not just into hand), so the "reach" can be staged as a full ritual/summoning rather than a quick gesture.

**Per-color notes:**
- **Black** (tutoring as bargaining with something looming and dangerous): the same infernal-reach register as the instant version, but sorcery speed allows the looming figure to be fully shown rather than half-glimpsed.
- **Green** (tutoring as ritual summons, when fetching a creature directly onto the battlefield): a full ring of supplicant/tribal figures calling something forth from the earth or void, the found creature emerging mid-summon rather than already standing.
- **White** (tutoring as invoking sanctioned lore): consistent with the instant version — a single lit tome/relic-shelf, one item in hard focus.

Blue is omitted here by design, not oversight: blue tutoring is overwhelmingly instant-speed in this sample (Mystical Tutor, etc.) — see the Instant skill's Tutor section for that register.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Black, based on Demonic Tutor.)* A robed figure reaches upward with both hands toward a massive horned shape looming out of red-black haze above her, her own upturned face lit only by a faint blue glow rising from her own open palms — the power flowing down to her, not yet grasped.
2. *(Green, based on Green Sun's Zenith.)* A towering plant-and-bone colossus erupts from a glowing green fissure in the earth at the center of a loose ring of small silhouetted figures, the ring's outer members still mid-approach, the colossus's head not yet fully clear of the ground.
3. *(White, based on Idyllic Tutor.)* A single enchanted relic sits lit by one clean shaft of light on an otherwise shadowed shelf of identical-looking objects, a gloved hand just entering frame toward it.

## Pump / Protection

**Decisive moment:** rare as a sorcery-speed role — the value of a combat trick usually depends on instant-speed surprise, so most of a deck's pump/protection effects belong in the Instant skill. The one sorcery-speed register that recurred in the sample was a *standing, army-wide buff* (an anthem-style burst affecting every creature the caster controls at once) rather than a single-target trick — use this register specifically when the card's effect is genuinely board-wide, not as a default sorcery pump treatment.

**Per-color note:** sampled from green only (Triumph of the Hordes). Treat as a single-color starting hypothesis.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Green, based on Triumph of the Hordes.)* A green shockwave of light crosses an entire advancing line of creatures simultaneously, each one's muscles visibly swelling and a faint toxic sheen spreading across their claws and teeth in the same instant, the wave's leading edge still visibly travelling past the frame's foreground figures toward the back of the line.

## Reanimation

**Decisive moment:** the returned creature caught in the *act* of rising or re-forming — earth or grave-matter still falling away, the body not yet fully resolved into its final solid state. Sorcery speed is where reanimation's primary visual identity lives (the instant-speed version is rare and more haunting/incomplete by comparison — see the Instant skill).

**Per-color note:** black, overwhelmingly — reanimation as a role belongs almost entirely to black. White has a rare mass-reanimation variant (Return to the Light Realms) worth noting as a contrast case: white's version reads as restoration/resurrection (warm, welcoming light) rather than black's version (cold, ritual, faintly wrong).

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Black, based on Dread Return.)* A massive horned, bone-white creature erupts up out of a cracked icy chasm, ice and grave-dirt still sliding off its shoulders and jaw as green ritual light spills from the fissure below it, a small robed figure in the foreground still mid-gesture with the summoning not yet complete.
2. *(White, contrast case based on Return to the Light Realms.)* Multiple translucent, golden-lit figures rise simultaneously from a single point of warm light breaking through storm clouds, each one solidifying from the head downward as if being poured back into existence rather than clawing their way up from below.

## Burn

**Decisive moment:** as with the instant version, damage caught mid-transit or at impact — but sorcery-speed burn in the sample skewed toward large-scale, multi-target damage (X-damage spells, damage-to-each-creature effects) rather than single precise bolts, since sorcery speed removes the ambush value a small burn spell would otherwise trade on.

**Per-color note:** red only. Where a red board wipe and red burn overlap mechanically (damage-to-each-creature effects), treat it under Board Wipe above if the card is explicitly a sweeper, and under this section if it's a single/few-target spell that merely happens to deal damage rather than clear the board.

**Worked examples:**
1. *(Based on Immolating Gyre / Crackle with Power register.)* A caster stands with both arms raised as multiple thin lances of white-orange energy fan outward simultaneously from a single point above their head, each lance already crossing open ground toward a different distant target, the caster's own silhouette staying dark against the light they're generating.
2. *(Based on classic single-bolt burn, e.g. Lightning Bolt's visual register.)* A jagged bolt of white-hot lightning is caught mid-arc between a storm-dark sky and a cracked, scorched impact point on the ground, the bolt's own light the only source illuminating the scene, everything past its immediate glow lost to black.
