**Battle is a very new, very small card type.** It was introduced in *March of the Machine* (2023) as the "Siege" subtype and has barely been reused since. A Scryfall search for `t:battle year>=2010 -is:digital -is:promo`, `unique=art`, returns **36 total unique arts**, effectively all of them the "Invasion of `<plane>`" cycle from that one set (plus its *Innistrad Remastered* reprint of the Innistrad card, set `inr`, verified via the Scryfall set API as a Masters reprint set, not a crossover). This file's conventions are grounded in five of those 36 viewed directly (Zendikar, Ikoria, Kaladesh, Amonkhet, Innistrad) — a representative slice of a genuinely thin, single-cycle corpus, not an exhaustive read. Treat this file's confidence accordingly: there is essentially one visual template in the entire card type's history, not a range of established sub-modes to choose between.

**Aspect-ratio override — read before using art-template Rule 9's closing suffix.** Every battle card sampled has a markedly panoramic art crop (roughly 3:1 wide, versus the ordinary card's roughly 4:3 art box). Rule 9's default `--ar 4:3` does not apply here — a Battle card's physical shape is genuinely different (it's printed landscape, rotated 90° from a normal card). **Default to `--ar 3:1`**, matching the sampled crop ratio, and state it explicitly in the prompt rather than relying on the shared default; use `--ar 16:9` only as a documented fallback (e.g. if a specific image tool rejects 3:1), not as an equally-valid first choice — picking one default consistently matters more than which one, so a deck's battle cards don't end up cropped to inconsistent ratios.

**Corrected 2026-09-24** — per art-template Rule 5, don't pre-crop or fade content toward the edges expecting the app's frame to eat into it; provide the full siege scene contained within the 3:1 frame, an even margin of background/environment around the edges, nothing cropped. The physical dividing-line device described below (a wall, a rampart, a bridge span) gives the composition a natural horizontal anchor across the wide canvas — keep both the attacking and defending sides, and that dividing line, fully visible within the frame rather than bleeding off any edge.

## Mode 1 — Contested-Territory Moment

**When it applies:** every battle card, without exception — there is no second mode to branch to at this sample depth.

**Decisive moment:** the siege or assault caught mid-motion, camera positioned so both the attacking side and the defending side read as present in the same frame rather than showing one army's charge in isolation. Per art-template's Who/What/Why/Where brief, the "Who" here is not one figure but two implied factions in the same shot; "What" is the specific instant of a wall being breached, a bridge being crossed, or an aerial assault meeting a ground defense — never a static aftermath and never a single hero's portrait (that framing belongs to `creature` or `planeswalker`, not `battle`). "Why" should map to the specific transformation the card's own text implies: a battle Siege always flips into something (a creature, an enchantment, a vehicle) once "defeated," so the art can hint at what's being fought over or what's about to emerge, without depicting the transformed side literally.

A recurring structural device across the sample: a physical dividing line in the composition — a fortress wall, a rampart, a bridge span, a line of raised weapons — with one side's forces pressing against or spilling over it from the other. This device does double duty: it reads instantly as "siege," and it gives Rule 5's framing band a natural real object to sit on (the wall or bridge line itself), rather than requiring an invented focal point.

**Diegetic lighting at this scale:** with two full sides of a conflict in frame rather than one figure, Rule 2's light source is usually environmental and shared by the whole scene — a break in storm clouds, a magical blast lighting one flank, a burning structure — rather than a single hand-held glow. When one side's magic and the other's differ in color or texture (a green territory's growth-magic vs. a red invader's fire), state that distinction explicitly per Rule 2's two-effects clause, since a two-army composition is exactly the case where two visual registers colliding is most likely to blur together if left unstated.

**Palette:** still capped at 2-3 named colors per Rule 4. Because two sides are in frame, the natural approach is one palette anchored to the defending territory's home color(s) with one contrasting accent color for the invading force's magic — e.g. "moss-green and bone-white, one violet-black intrusion" — rather than doubling up to a 4-5 color list to cover both sides literally.

### Worked examples

1. *(Zendikar under assault — colorless/multicolor invaders vs. a green-white land, based on Invasion of Zendikar.)* A jagged sunlit hedron-topped plateau splits the frame at its lower third, defenders' small silhouettes braced along its rim as skeletal void-black flying invaders and drifting debris storm in low from the right, a churning bruise-purple and red-veined sky pressing down from above as the shared light source, its cold pale glow crisping the plateau's stone edge while the invaders' silhouettes stay flat black against it. Palette: sun-bleached stone gold, storm-cloud violet-grey, void-black silhouettes.
2. *(Ikoria under assault — green bio-horror vs. red/black invaders, based on Invasion of Ikoria.)* A wall of jagged green bone-and-chitin monstrosities rears up on the left, facing a mirrored wall of red-black barbed, spined horrors surging in from the right, the two masses meeting at a bright vertical seam of clashing light dead-center where their opposing magics visibly collide without blending — green kept crisp and angular, red-black kept molten and rounded — pale sky bleeding through the gap between them as the frame's brightest point. Palette: acid green, blood-red, near-black.
3. *(Kaladesh under assault — artifact-plane skyline vs. an unseen aerial force, based on Invasion of Kaladesh.)* A brass-and-glass domed spire hovers at the center of a night skyline studded with slender clockwork towers, twin flares of hard white weapon-light bursting at the frame's far left and right edges as unseen attackers close in, faint lightning arcing down from the dome to the towers below it as the scene's secondary light source — the dome's own soft internal glow is warmer and steadier than the cold white flares bracketing it, keeping the two light registers distinct per Rule 2. Palette: deep violet-blue night, brass-gold dome light, stark white flare accents.
4. *(Amonkhet under assault — desert god-city vs. an aerial incursion, based on Invasion of Amonkhet.)* Three towering jackal- and hawk-headed god-figures loom over a sun-baked pyramid rampart lined with spiked siege engines, smoke and flying debris streaking in from above as dark winged shapes dive toward the wall, a hazy violet-grey dust-storm light washing evenly over the whole scene and flattening the god-figures into near-silhouette against the pale sky behind them. Palette: sun-bleached sandstone gold, dust violet-grey, near-black silhouette.
5. *(Innistrad under assault — human/angel defenders vs. a horde of wolves and undead, based on Invasion of Innistrad.)* A line of armored, spear-braced defenders and a rearing bear hold a shadowed foreground rampart as a wall of red-eyed wolves and pale zombie-horde figures crash against it from the right, a cold, moon-bright glow breaking through storm cloud directly above the clash point as the shared light source, throwing hard silver highlights on raised spears and pale fur while the crowd's outer edges recede into warm firelit haze at both sides. Palette: moonlit silver-blue, warm ember-orange haze, near-black shadow.

**Sampling note — genuinely thin, honestly disclosed:** all five worked examples above are drawn from a single design cycle (Invasion of `<plane>`, *March of the Machine*) reused once as a reprint; battle as a card type has essentially no independent second cycle to cross-check this mode against as of this sample. There is no meaningful color-driven sub-mode split the way `land` or `planeswalker` support — every example follows the same "two sides meeting at a dividing line" structure regardless of color, so color is noted per example as a detail (per this file's own instruction, colors pooled rather than split) rather than a distinct branch. If a future battle cycle introduces a materially different composition (a naval siege, a single-defender duel, a bombardment with no visible defending side), this file's single mode should be revisited rather than stretched to cover it by analogy.
