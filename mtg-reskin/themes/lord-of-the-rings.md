# Theme Bible — Lord of the Rings

Built per `skills/theme-bible/SKILL.md`. Lord of the Rings is this skill's clean worked-example theme: five colors map to five distinguishable factions with little forcing, so this bible does not need the adaptation section's factionless/facet-based handling — every section below reads as a direct political/cultural faction mapping.

## 1. Color → faction/region map

- **White** — Gondor and the light-aligned Elves (Rivendell's Noldorin lords, Lothlórien's Galadhrim at their most radiant): order, stewardship, and a defense of the world's remaining light against the long defeat — white's protection/order register fits both a failing kingdom holding a line and an elder people holding back decay through sheer will and memory.
- **Blue** — Rivendell as a seat of counsel, the Istari (wizards) as an order, and the Sea as the direction all deep knowledge in Middle-earth eventually points toward (Círdan's Havens, Ulmo's ocean-lore, the elves' own sea-longing): blue's knowledge/manipulation register fits a faction defined by foresight, patient counsel, and quiet influence over open conflict.
- **Black** — Mordor, the Nazgûl, and the Barrow-downs' restless dead: domination through corruption and a will to power that consumes its own servants — black's register of ambition at any cost fits Sauron's court exactly, and the Barrow-wights extend it to what corruption leaves behind once the corrupter has moved on.
- **Red** — the Dwarves, the Balrog, and Middle-earth's dragons: a shared register of fire, forge, and buried appetite — dwarvish craft is inseparable from the forge-fire that makes it, the Balrog is fire given a body, and a dragon's hoard-guarding greed is red's impulsive, possessive intensity turned into a survival strategy rather than a virtue.
- **Green** — the Ents, the Shire, and Rohan's plains: growth, endurance, and a shared reluctance to enter a war until it is already at the door — Ents rouse only when Isengard fells their trees, the Shire stays insular until the War of the Ring reaches its own borders, and Rohan rides only once Théoden throws off Wormtongue's paralysis. Green here is patience under provocation, not passivity.
- **Multicolor** — alliances and border regions where two of the above meet: Aragorn's claim spanning Gondor and the Dúnedain Rangers, the Rohan-Gondor beacon-alliance, Saruman's betrayal of the White Council pulling Isengard toward Mordor's register. Multicolor is not a sixth faction — it is "which two mapped factions are in tension or alliance here," resolved per deck.
- **Colorless** — the Rings of Power, palantíri, and other ancient relics and forgotten works predating the present factions: their danger is precisely that they answer to no faction's values, only to whoever currently holds them.

## 2. Card type → motif map

- **Artifacts** → forged relics and made objects: the One Ring and the lesser Rings, Andúril reforged from Narsil, palantíri, mithril-coats passed down through lineages — an artifact's power in this theme is always a function of who wields or inherits it, not an inert stat stick.
- **Enchantments** → oaths, wards, and curses laid on people or places: the Oath of the Dead binding the Army of the Dead to Isildur's heir, the ward over the Ford of Bruinen, Saruman's voice-corruption of Théoden, Galadriel's protective enchantment over Lothlórien's borders.
- **Sagas** → a chapter of Middle-earth's own established chronology told as a three-beat vignette: "The Council of Elrond," "The Scouring of the Shire," "The Fall of Isengard" — each saga should read as a scene lifted from the legendarium's own timeline, not an invented event.
- **Lands** → named locations drawn from Middle-earth's actual geography, never a generic terrain label: Fangorn, the Pelennor Fields, the Argonath, Weathertop, the Paths of the Dead, Bag End.
- **Instants/Sorceries** → a single decisive act performed by a specific named person or force, not an ambient magic system: Gandalf breaking the bridge at Khazad-dûm, Éowyn's blade meeting the Witch-king, Frodo invoking Eärendil's light against Shelob — per `naming/SKILL.md` Rule 2, the theme supplies both a specific actor and a specific deed for each spell effect.
- **Creatures** → see Section 3, the subtype translation table.
- **Planeswalkers** → the setting's own named, larger-than-life figures: Gandalf, Galadriel, Sauron, Saruman, Aragorn at his most legendary — Middle-earth has an unusually rich supply of these, so this type needs no substitute or adaptation note.

## 3. Subtype translation table

- **Angel → unfallen Maiar in physical, winged manifestation** (messenger-spirits of Valinor's light, closer to Melian's kind than to any fallen Maia): keep the paired wings, the raised weapon-tipped staff/polearm, and the halo-adjacent light exactly as `creature/SKILL.md` specifies; reskin the armor/material to Middle-earth's forged-metal-and-woven-cloth register rather than a generic celestial one.
- **Demon → the Balrog and its lesser kin**: keep the oversized gaping mouth, the asymmetric skull-horns, and the visible internal fire-glow/veining unchanged (a Balrog's fire-within is the same signature feature, made literal); translate the wings and whip-and-sword iconography to the Balrog's own shadow-and-flame register.
- **Devil → the sly informant-orcs and spies of Sauron's court** (the Mouth of Sauron's underlings, crebain-handlers, Isengard's spies): keep the small-to-human scale, the close-lipped/smirking clever expression, and the social/character prop (a captured token carried as a taunt); translate the backswept horns to jagged iron helm-spikes rather than literal horns.
- **Vampire → corrupted Black Númenórean lords and the Nazgûl before their fall into wraith-form**: keep the aristocratic portrait framing, the pale sharp-cheekboned face beneath a widow's-peak or centrally-parted hairline, and the crescent-moon/night-sky backdrop; translate the blood-trickle-at-the-mouth signature to a visible corruption-mark — a black vein creeping from the throat where a Ring's power has taken hold — since literal blood-drinking has no equivalent in this theme's aristocratic-corruption register.
- **Zombie → the Barrow-wights and the Dead Men of Dunharrow before Aragorn releases them**: keep the localized-rot/patchy-discoloration signature feature and the graveyard-prop cluster; translate the props from a modern graveyard to a barrow-mound with ancient grave-goods, or the oath-hall beneath the Dwimorberg.
- **Skeleton → the released Army of the Dead marching to the Stone of Erech and the Pelennor**: keep the fully exposed skull, the bone-colored jointed limbs, and regalia draped over bare bone (ancient Gondorian or Dunlending war-gear); translate the "royalty of the dead" reading to specifically oath-broken kings, not generic undead royalty.
- **Spirit → the fading, non-hostile presences of Middle-earth's own twilight**: the fen-lights of the Dead Marshes, or an Elf's fading memory-echo as the Third Age closes: keep the dissolving vapor-edge silhouette, the palette that blends into its setting, and the faint afterimage/out-of-phase quality; if a deck's sample never surfaces a card needing this softer register, fold it into the Zombie or Skeleton entry's translation instead, since Middle-earth's undead skew toward the more violent registers those two entries cover.
- **Horror → Ungoliant's brood and the Watcher in the Water**: keep the single dominant maw as visual anchor, the matched limb/tentacle pairs arranged radially, and the fused mismatched-parts body; translate the setting-specific register to a drowned, ancient-evil-of-the-deep aesthetic rather than a cosmic/alien one.
- **Eldrazi → Morgoth-era horrors surfacing from Utumno and Ungoliant's own Unlight**: keep the titanic radially-symmetric tentacle-radiating build, the total absence of a legible face, and the void/lightning-torn background; translate the "cosmic origin" detail to the First Age's own pre-Sun darkness rather than an alien void.
- **Phyrexian → Isengard's crossbred Uruk-hai fused with siege-iron, and Sauron's ring-forged augmentations**: keep the bilateral human-derived chassis with blades grown directly from the forearm, the smooth featureless mask, and the exposed muscle between metal plates; translate the "compleation" process to Saruman's breeding-pits and forge-grafts rather than an oil-based biomechanical contagion.
- **Dragon → the literal dragons of Middle-earth** (Smaug, Glaurung, Ancalagon the Black): keep every signature feature unchanged — horn/frill crown, overlapping individual scales, the open-mouthed roar/breath-weapon pose — this is a genuine one-to-one match needing no translation beyond naming the specific dragon and its hoard.
- **Sphinx → the animate stone Watchers of Cirith Ungol and Moria's guardian-carvings given voice**: keep the perfectly mirrored wing-halves as a static compositional frame, the radially symmetric crested headdress, and the serpentine/lion-derived tail below a fused humanoid-beast torso; translate the riddle-posing role to a guardian-oath role (the Watchers challenge trespassers rather than posing literal riddles).
- **Hydra → the many-necked brood-serpents of the Dead Marshes and Mordor's fens**: keep the multiple independently-posed heads sharing one body, the dripping venom/ichor, and a faint per-head color cue; translate the setting register from an open-water lake-monster to a fen/marsh-dwelling horror.
- **Elemental → the primal river- and earth-spirits Middle-earth's own eldest magic still answers to** (the flood-spirit of the Bruinen roused by Elrond and Gandalf against the Nazgûl, living root-and-stone spirits of the world's first ages): keep the mask-like face that is the whole head, the home substance rendered as literal body material (real water, real stone), and the ornate symmetrical regalia; translate the "civilization" detail to the world's own primeval age rather than any people's craft.
- **Beast → the bred and twisted monstrosities of Isengard and Mordor** (outsized Wargs, wolves of Angband's bloodline bred larger and crueler): keep the composite, assembled-not-evolved anatomy, the oversized disproportionate maw, and integrated growths; translate the crystalline/mechanical growths to crude iron studs and grafts bred into the animal rather than a sci-fi material.
- **Dinosaur → the Mûmakil of Far Harad and Middle-earth's elder-day megafauna** (the great elk and cave-bears of Beorn's kin): keep the pack/paired staging and the anatomically-real, non-invented body plan; translate the theropod-specific tail/spine cue loosely to whichever real-animal lineage the specific beast draws from (elephantine for Mûmakil, ursine for Beorn's kin), since Middle-earth's giant fauna isn't uniformly theropod-shaped.
- **Elf → the Elves of Middle-earth directly**: a rare one-to-one translation. Keep every signature feature (tapering ears clear of the hairline, living plant material worked into garments, a woodland-crown accessory) unchanged, since the corpus convention and this theme's own source material already agree.
- **Goblin → the Orcs and goblins of Middle-earth directly** (Moria's goblin-host, Mordor's rank-and-file orcs): another near one-to-one case. Keep the underbite grin with mismatched teeth, the jury-rigged scrap gear, and the pack/mob staging around a boss-figure unchanged — Tolkien's own goblins already match this corpus convention closely.
- **Merfolk → Ulmo's ocean-Maiar (Ossë, Uinen) and the sea-attuned Elves of the Falas and the Grey Havens**: keep the aquatic-modified lower body, the fin-crested head, and the coral/pearl jewelry worked into hair or collar; translate the scale texture to mother-of-pearl and worked shell rather than literal fish-scale, since Tolkien's sea-elves are seafarers rather than aquatic-bodied beings.
- **Faerie → the small Silvan light-sprites of Lothlórien's mallorn canopy**: keep the translucent, vein-patterned stained-glass wings, the antenna-like hair wisps, and the found-object scale cue (a mallorn leaf or dewdrop as furniture); this is an invented equivalent, since Tolkien's own Silvan Elves aren't miniaturized — the translation borrows only the diminutive nature-spirit register, not the Elf subtype's own people.
- **Giant → the stone-giants of the Misty Mountains and the cave-trolls of the Ettenmoors**: keep the explicit scale-anchor staging against ordinary-sized figures, the crude improvised weapons (a thrown boulder), and the rough weathered stone-gray skin — this is close to a one-to-one match, since Tolkien's own stone-giants already fit this signature-feature set.
- **Dwarf → the Dwarves of Middle-earth directly**: the same one-to-one case as Elf. Keep the long braided beard with metal clasps, the segmented tooled-metalwork plate, and the single iconic hafted weapon (war-axe or hammer) unchanged.
- **Human — Knight → the Swan Knights of Dol Amroth and the household knights of Gondor**: keep the tall crested great-helm, the heraldic surcoat marked with a personal sigil (the swan-ship of Dol Amroth), and the drawn longsword held vertically at guard.
- **Human — Soldier → the Tower Guard of Minas Tirith and Rohan's levied fyrd**: keep the uniform tabard in house colors (black-and-silver for Gondor), the weapon lowered/shouldered rather than mid-swing, and the background formation cues.
- **Human — Cleric → the healers of the Houses of Healing** (Ioreth and her fellow healers tending the wounded after the Battle of the Pelennor Fields): keep the refined embroidered garment and the direct physical closeness to a tended being — here reframed from a monstrous/divine being to a wounded soldier or dying king — and the calm, intimate expression; this is the theme's nearest working substitute, since Middle-earth has no organized priesthood for a literal cleric-of-a-god reading.
- **Human — Wizard → the Istari themselves** (Gandalf, Saruman, Radagast): a near one-to-one translation. Keep the floating/hand-drawn glyph or portal, the arcane focus staff, and robes caught mid-motion as if wind-whipped by the spell itself.
- **Human — Rogue → hobbit burglars and Bree's shadowed underworld** (Bilbo and Frodo as literal "burglars," Barliman Butterbur's inn as the setting register): keep the dynamic mid-motion pose and the tattered, travel-worn cloak; translate the literal-mask/disguise-iconography signature feature to the Ring's own invisibility (a hobbit half-faded from view) rather than a physical mask, and the neon-cityscape setting cue to Bree's lantern-lit, rain-slick lanes.
- **Human — Warrior → the Dúnedain Rangers of the North** (Aragorn's own kin before he claims Gondor's throne): keep the lean, upright, confident pose and a slim blade held loosely rather than a heavy weapon; translate the domino/eye-mask signature feature to the Rangers' own deep-hooded, weathered grey cloaks obscuring the face, and the dramatic collar/cape to that same grey cloak.
- **Human — Shaman → the woad-marked ritualists of the Drúadan Forest (the Wild Men) and Dunland's hedge-shamans**: keep the ragged minimal clothing baring skin, the bone/tooth jewelry worn against bare skin, and the hunched, grounded ritual posture; translate the flame/spectral-energy signature feature to old curses and hill-magic predating Rohan's arrival in Calenardhon.
- **Human — Noble → the Stewards of Gondor's court in the White Tower and Rohan's court at Meduseld**: keep the crown or circlet as explicit rank-marker, the fur-trimmed cloak or ceremonial collar, and the throne/dais/retinue framing (Denethor's black-and-white hall, Théoden's golden hall).
- **Human — Assassin → the Black Númenórean agents and poisoners in Sauron's direct service** (Gríma Wormtongue's own poisoner's-craft is the nearest in-canon analogue): keep the personal-sigil facial marking, here reframed as a brand of the Lidless Eye, the blade held low and casually, and the unbothered, detached expression at or after a violent act.
- **Construct → the Dwarven forge-automata of Khazad-dûm and ancient Númenórean sentinel-statues**: keep the exposed jointed limb segmentation with visible hinge/rivet lines, the glowing inset gem or lens-like "eyes," and the ornate engraved metalwork marking a specific civilization's craft (Dwarven runework, or Númenórean stonecraft).
- **Golem → sun-petrified stone-trolls and the crude clay-and-stone guardians of ancient ruins**: keep the blocky monolithic single-material build, the single glowing core or seam of visible energy, and the crude scale-mismatch against nearby humans; the Bert-Tom-William stone trolls of The Hobbit are this theme's own canonical starting point for the "single raw material" signature feature.
- **Insect → Shelob's brood and the giant spiders of Mirkwood**: keep the compound/faceted eyes dominating the head and the chitinous segmented plating (translated to a spider's own iridescent, jewel-toned carapace rather than an insect's); the wing-membrane signature feature has no plausible occurrence for this theme's giant spiders and should be dropped rather than forced — flag any deck that surfaces a genuinely winged giant-insect card (Mordor's carrion-flies near the Dead Marshes) as the one case where the full three-feature set applies.

## 4. Naming vocabulary

**Language flavor:** this theme draws on (at least) four distinct registers, each carrying Tolkien's own invented or attested linguistic material rather than generic fantasy names: Sindarin and Quenya (Elvish, ornate/ceremonial, vowel-heavy and soft-consonant), Old English phonology (Rohan/Rohirric, formal/archaic, hard consonants and horse-and-oath vocabulary), Khuzdul-flavored Dwarvish (clipped, hard-consonant, title-of-craft heavy), and plain Westron/Common Speech (Gondor's court register and the Shire's homely, unpretentious register at opposite ends of the same language).

**Naming patterns:** patronymics ("son of," rendered directly — Théoden King, son of Thengel), epithets tied to a deed or place ("Aragorn, Elessar," "the White Rider," "Shelob's Bane"), title-plus-name constructions ("Faramir, Steward of Gondor"), and compound descriptive names built from meaningful roots rather than arbitrary syllables (Rivendell = "deep valley," Isengard = "iron enclosure").

**Roots and recurring elements:**
- Sindarin/Quenya: *-(i)el* / *-iel* (daughter-of; Arwen Undómiel), *aran-/ar-* (king/noble; Aragorn, Arwen), *-dor* (land; Gondor, Mordor), *galad-/gil-* (light/star; Galadriel, Gildor), *thranduil-/-nduil* (flower/foam compounds).
- Rohirric (Old English-flavored): *éo-* (horse; Éomer, Éowyn, Éomund), *-wine* (friend), *-helm/-ric* (protection/rule; Théodred, Aldric), epithets tied to horses, plains, and oaths ("Rider of the Mark," "Marshal of the Riddermark").
- Dwarvish (Khuzdul-flavored, used sparingly and mostly for place/hall names per Tolkien's own practice, with day-to-day names drawn from Old Norse–flavored "outer names" instead): *-in/-im* endings (Durin, Thorin, Balin), hall-names built as "the [Adjective] Halls" (the Ancient Halls, the Iron Hills).
- Westron/Common Speech (Gondor court and the Shire, same base language at different registers): Gondor favors elevated, title-bearing constructions ("Steward of Gondor," "Captain of the White Tower"); the Shire favors homely, food-and-land-rooted surnames (Baggins, Took, Brandybuck) and unpretentious place-names (Bag End, Hobbiton).

**Per-faction register (feeds `naming/SKILL.md` Rule 5):**
- Gondor (White): elevated, formal, title-heavy Westron — "Steward's Vigil," "The White Tower's Oath," "Boromir's Last Stand."
- Rivendell/wizards/sea (Blue): Sindarin/Quenya vowel-heavy ceremony — "Light of Imladris," "The Council's Counsel," "Vilya's Ward."
- Mordor/Nazgûl/Barrow (Black): harsh, corrupted, or hollowed-out titles stripped of the name they once had — "The Nine's Hunger," "Herald of the Lidless Eye," "The Wight's Claim."
- Dwarves/Balrog/dragons (Red): clipped hard-consonant craft-titles and hoard-language — "Dáin's Reckoning," "The Forge-Oath," "Smaug's Hoard-Wrath."
- Ents/Shire/Rohan (Green): a deliberate split within one color — Ent names run slow and compound ("Fangorn's Patience," "Treebeard's Muster"), Shire names run homely and food-rooted ("The Old Took's Pipe-Weed," "Bag End's Last Party"), Rohirric names run Old-English-flavored and horse/oath-bound ("Éomund's Vow," "Rider of the Mark," "Oath at the Hornburg") — a batch call should check a new Green-identity name against whichever of the three sub-registers that specific card's subtype cluster points to, not treat Green as one undifferentiated voice.

## 5. Deck anchor guidance

The commander's own color identity and subtypes point to which of Section 1's mapped factions is the deck's anchor, and every other detected faction in that specific deck should be described in the deck lore doc in terms of its relationship *to* the anchor — not as a free-floating faction with no stated connection to the commander.

This matters more for this theme than most, because the same card pool reads entirely differently depending on which faction is home: a **Fellowship-anchored** deck (commander drawn from Gondor, Rivendell, or a Fellowship-spanning multicolor identity) reads Mordor, the Nazgûl, and the Barrow-downs as the opposition to be resisted — every black card in that deck is a threat bearing down on the anchor. A **Sauron/Mordor-anchored** deck (commander drawn from Black) inverts the same card pool entirely: Gondor and Rohan become the opposition, the Nazgûl become the anchor's own loyal servants, and even a card as unambiguous as "the One Ring" reads as the anchor's own creation and goal rather than a burden being carried away from it. Nothing about the underlying card pool changes between these two readings — only which faction the deck lore doc treats as "home" changes, and that choice cascades into how every other faction in the deck gets described.

A commander whose color identity spans a mapped alliance (Section 1's multicolor entry — e.g. an Aragorn-identity commander spanning Gondor and the Dúnedain Rangers) anchors the deck on that alliance itself, not on an arbitrary pick of one of its component colors.

## 6. Inter-faction relations default

This theme defaults to **theme-canon-first**, per the skill's default rule — Lord of the Rings has an unusually well-documented answer for how nearly every mapped faction relates to every other, so invented relationships should be rare here.

**Theme-canon relationships (cited):**
- **Gondor ↔ Rohan (White ↔ Green):** sworn allies under the Oath of Eorl — Rohan rides to Gondor's defense at the Pelennor Fields, and the alliance is a load-bearing plot point of the whole War of the Ring.
- **Gondor/Rivendell ↔ Mordor (White/Blue ↔ Black):** unambiguous, longstanding adversaries — the Last Alliance of Elves and Men was fought against Sauron directly, and Gondor's entire founding purpose (per its own lore) is to watch Mordor's border.
- **The Elves ↔ the Dwarves (Blue ↔ Red):** historically wary and occasionally hostile (the Silmarillion-era conflict at Menegroth, Thranduil's imprisonment of Thorin's company), thawing only late and personally through Legolas and Gimli's friendship — a deck should default to wariness as the baseline register unless the specific deck's story is explicitly about that late thaw.
- **The Ents ↔ Rohan/the Shire (Green internal):** the Ents' march on Isengard is fought in Rohan's direct interest (Saruman's betrayal threatens both), making this an alliance-of-circumstance within the same color rather than a true separate relationship.
- **The Shire's relative isolation:** canon-established — the hobbits stay insular and largely uninvolved in the wider world's politics until the War of the Ring reaches their own borders in "The Scouring of the Shire," which should inform how a Shire-flavored card relates to the other Green sub-registers (distant rather than close, until provoked).
- **Rivendell's withdrawal:** the Elves of the Third Age are canonically fading and departing rather than actively contesting anyone — Blue's relationship to conflict in general should read as reluctant counsel and eventual departure (the Grey Havens), not active aggression.
- **The Barrow-wights ↔ Mordor/the Nazgûl (Black internal):** Appendix A ("The Realms in Exile") states that after Arnor's fall, evil spirits sent by the Witch-king of Angmar — chief of the Nazgûl and Sauron's principal lieutenant — entered the abandoned barrow-mounds and became the Barrow-wights. This is a documented origin tying them directly to the Nazgûl's chain of command, not an independent evil: a deck should treat the Barrow-wights as Angmar/Nazgûl-derived agents, dormant and localized rather than actively directed day-to-day, but not severed from Mordor's command structure.

**Invented relationships (canon-silent, flagged as such):**
- **The Ents ↔ the Dwarves (Green ↔ Red):** Middle-earth's canon has little to say about direct Ent-Dwarf interaction — the two barely appear in scenes together. This bible invents a plausible register for a deck that needs one: mutual mistrust rooted in the Dwarves' historical mining and felling of trees near Moria and the Ents' long memory for exactly that kind of harm, without either side having a specific canonical grievance to point to.
